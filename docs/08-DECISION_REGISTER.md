## Locked cross-system rules

- All content and progression must remain solo-completable.
- Player skill, equipment, and systems knowledge all matter; none should completely replace the others.
- Endgame intensity is allowed to be extreme, but readability is non-negotiable.
- Grind is acceptable and desirable when deliberate, targetable, cumulative, and rewarding.
- The ten-second, ten-minute, and one-hour loops are representative windows, not required encounter, activity, or session durations.
- Shorter loops may focus on one pursuit or mix naturally as opportunities emerge; they do not require every possible action or a guaranteed completion or upgrade.
- The central long-term arc is zero-to-hero growth into a substantial endgame, not completion of a fixed campaign.
- A session does not need to award a permanent upgrade to be successful; self-chosen progress, learning, execution, and relevant attempts can make it worthwhile.
- Repeated targeted loot attempts are meaningful across the pursuit, but rolls remain independent: failures do not raise the next drop chance or bank pity under the CORE-16 baseline.
- Active single-player play is pausable.
- An ordinary dungeon is normally one committed instance. Leaving or abandoning ends it; partial room-by-room completion is not resumed across entries.
- Important rewards must be personally obtainable without trading.
- Core progression must not depend on real-time waiting, scheduled obligations, or fear of missing out.
- Uniques are authored specializations, not automatic best-in-slot replacements.
- Optional supporting activities may provide substantial collection grinds without becoming pillars or required power progression; foraging and fishing are the first accepted examples.
- Limited crafting may convert foraging, fishing, and other explicit requirements into known optional non-combat rewards, including high-dedication non-combat mounts, skins, cosmetics, and collection rewards. It must not create or modify combat equipment, combat power, consumables, or buffs, and must not become a sprawling material economy or broad profession.
- Collectables should primarily live in dedicated, polished interface menus with clear progress and completion feedback rather than requiring physical displays.
- Player housing is not currently planned and is not needed for collection, storage, crafting, or progression. It is reconsiderable only if a separate compelling purpose emerges.
- Settlements remain authored locations rather than player-built or managed systems; selected authored changes may occur through quests, major victories, or world events.
- Pets, if included, are purely cosmetic collection companions with no combat or statistic benefit; they never attack, draw aggro, tank, cast abilities, or function as party members.
- Fishing and foraging are the only non-combat activities currently planned with their own progression levels. Limited crafting is not a leveled profession, and no wider profession roster is planned.
- Outdoor zones are not level-locked. Danger is their main natural readiness barrier, while selected contained locations may still use meaningful hard gates.
- Dungeons, raids, and selected challenge instances use explicit minimum-level entry requirements. Meeting the requirement permits an attempt but does not guarantee readiness or success.
- Access requirements must be clear, purposeful, and proportionate. Denied entry never consumes a portal, key, or similar resource; permanent progression gates normally remain solved, while explicitly repeatable access items may still be required for each attempt.
- The world is layered across many large outdoor zone maps and separate interior maps. Neighbouring outdoor zones are reached through physical connections whose discovery and learning are part of world knowledge.
- Auto-travel is selective teleportation to eligible discovered and permanently unlocked destinations; it cannot reveal or bypass unknown locations, hidden connections, or the learned final routes to places outside the network.
- Auto-travel may begin from most safe outdoor locations but is blocked by combat, immediate danger, and contained maps. Its three-to-five-second cast is canceled by movement, damage, or combat.
- Each successful teleport costs currency, canceled casts cost nothing, and there is no separate free recall ability.
- World geography and progression-critical locations are authored. Controlled procedural variation may refresh repeatable details inside those authored spaces, but it must preserve world knowledge, targetability, and the identity of major dungeons and raids.
- Outdoor regions, outdoor enemies, and world bosses retain authored difficulty and never scale automatically to the player.
- Selected instanced dungeons may later offer separate higher-difficulty versions with fixed mechanics and rewards, but no version dynamically adapts to whoever enters.
- Combat is seamless, real-time, top-down, and freely aimed; movement and aiming are independent.
- Combat never requires a selected or locked target. Acquiring a target is never a prerequisite for aiming, attacking, using skills, dealing damage, or killing. Focus targeting is cut entirely: no focus-target system exists on any input method, enemy information comes from general interface presentation, and controller aim assistance (if any) works without a focus state.
- Combat resolution is primarily spatial. Hidden accuracy or evasion rolls cannot reject a visibly connected hit, although clearly communicated defenses may still prevent or alter damage.
- Tapping fires once, holding fires continuously, and a remappable autofire toggle simulates the held-attack state without selecting or aiming at enemies.
- Ordinary primary attacks are always available and consume no mana, stamina, ammunition, durability, or reload resource.
- Ordinary primary attacks preserve independent, predictable movement and aiming, and their authored firing patterns do not use random accuracy bloom or unexplained projectile drift.
- The equipped weapon defines the complete primary-attack behaviour. Ordinary projectiles interact predictably with visible solid terrain, and impact feedback must remain clear without obscuring precise dodging.
- Dodging is purely movement-based: no universal dash, roll, blink, sprint, block, parry, shield, or invulnerability-frame action. Every attack pattern must be dodgeable through movement alone by every class, and no encounter may require a specific class's mobility or defensive tool.
- Each character has one active ability, granted by the equipped class ability item; the skill tree grants no actives, and its meaningful nodes change behaviour rather than only numbers.
- No encounter may require a specific ability item—or any active ability—to be survivable; movement alone must remain sufficient.
- Difficulty escalates through projectile density, speed, pattern complexity, and encounter composition, never hit-point sponging; every zone carries its own local intensity spread within its authored band.
- The equipped loadout is four slots — weapon, ability item, armor, ring — and all pieces may carry statistics; behaviour beyond statistics belongs to weapon patterns, ability items, tree nodes, and uniques.
- No equipment slot may collapse into a single universally correct choice; armor and ring options require genuinely situational tradeoffs.
